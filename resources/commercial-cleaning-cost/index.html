Commercial Cleaning Costs in Tempe: Square Foot Pricing

A practical guide to how commercial cleaning is priced in the East Valley market—and what to look for when comparing bids.

Commercial cleaning cost in Tempe: what drives the number

Commercial cleaning in the Tempe and East Valley market is priced on four primary variables: square footage, cleaning frequency, space type and access window. Of these, frequency is the strongest single driver. Understanding how these interact will help you evaluate bids on equal terms rather than comparing a monthly cost figure that means different things across different proposals.

Square footage and the minimum charge

Square footage provides the baseline for commercial cleaning pricing, but it is not a linear multiplier below a certain threshold. Spaces under 1,500 square feet typically carry a minimum visit charge regardless of area, because the cost of scheduling, crew dispatch and supply preparation does not scale proportionally to very small footprints. Above roughly 2,500 square feet, pricing becomes more predictable per square foot as the fixed costs are absorbed.

In the Tempe market, the per-square-foot cost of nightly commercial cleaning for a standard mid-size office (5,000–15,000 sq ft) cleaned five nights per week is higher per square foot than the same facility cleaned once per week, even though it is the same building. This seems counterintuitive until you account for crew hours: five-night service requires a crew that cleans the space fully each visit, while weekly service allows less thorough attention per visit with a quarterly deep clean scheduled separately.

Frequency: the largest single variable

Frequency affects price more than any other single factor in a commercial cleaning contract. A 10,000 sq ft office cleaned Monday through Friday at standard commercial rates will cost between three and five times as much per month as the same office cleaned once per week. The ratio varies based on the cleaning tasks involved (restroom programs and trash removal cost the same whether done nightly or weekly; vacuuming costs are roughly proportional to frequency).

In the Tempe market, most mid-size professional offices settle on a three-night or five-night program. One-night-per-week programs are common for smaller offices (under 3,000 sq ft) and for spaces with low foot traffic. Facilities with public-facing operations, food service in the building or healthcare tenants almost always require five-night programs.

Space type and restroom count

Restrooms are the most labour-intensive space per square foot in any commercial building. A 200 sq ft multi-fixture restroom takes proportionally longer to clean correctly than 200 sq ft of open office. Each restroom adds meaningful time to a nightly scope. When comparing quotes, check whether the restroom count is explicitly included in the scope and whether supply replenishment (paper towel, toilet tissue, soap) is included in the price or billed at cost separately.

Space type also affects equipment requirements. A facility with polished concrete floors requires different equipment than a carpeted office. A building with a food service component requires different cleaning chemistry. These requirements affect the cost of supplying and equipping the crew, and they should be reflected in the quote.

Access window

After-hours access (6pm onward) is the standard window for nightly commercial cleaning. Access outside this window—mid-morning, during business hours, specific after-midnight windows—can add coordination cost. In Tempe, most commercial facilities have after-hours key fob or security code access, which is straightforward to manage. Buildings requiring a security escort or a specific arrival window narrower than two hours add crew coordination time.

Floor care pricing

Floor care programs (VCT strip-and-wax, polished concrete maintenance, carpet extraction) are typically priced separately from nightly cleaning, either as an add-on to a recurring contract or as standalone project work. Strip-and-wax pricing in the Tempe market depends on square footage, the number of coats to be applied, and whether the existing floor requires stripping before refinishing. Carpet extraction pricing depends on square footage and the degree of soiling.

What to check when comparing bids

Commercial cleaning bids in the Tempe market vary widely because they often describe different scopes. Before comparing the monthly total, confirm the following across every bid you receive:

  • Is the scope documented, or is it a generic "office cleaning" line item?
  • Is restroom supply replenishment included or billed separately?
  • What frequency does the price reflect? (Nightly, 3x week, weekly?)
  • Is floor care included in the price, or is it a separate contract?
  • Is the contractor insured and bonded? Can they produce a certificate of insurance immediately?

A bid that is 20% lower than the next-lowest quote is either covering less scope or using a lower cost structure that often reflects uninsured contractors, high crew turnover or inadequate equipment. In a nightly cleaning program, the consequences of a contractor who cannot perform consistently show up in your facility within 30 days.
