Insurance, Bonding and Why It Matters for Your Building

What to verify before signing a commercial cleaning contract in Tempe—and what happens when a contractor does not have the right coverage.

The insurance gap in commercial cleaning

Commercial cleaning has a lower barrier to entry than most commercial services. Starting a cleaning company requires minimal capital, no licence in most states, and no certification. The result is a market where coverage ranges from fully insured contractors with bonded and background-checked crews to solo operators with no insurance and no accountability structure whatsoever.

In the Tempe commercial cleaning market, the price difference between a fully insured contractor and an uninsured operator can be 20–40%. That price difference is real, and it reflects real cost. If you choose the lower-priced option without verifying coverage, you are accepting a contingent liability that does not appear on the invoice.

General liability insurance

General liability insurance covers third-party property damage and bodily injury arising from the contractor's operations. In the context of commercial cleaning, this means: if a crew member breaks a piece of equipment, damages a floor surface, or causes a trip hazard that results in an injury, the contractor's general liability policy is the first line of response.

Without general liability insurance, the liability for damages caused by a cleaning crew in your facility runs to your building insurance policy or, in some cases, to you personally as the party who engaged the contractor. Building owners and property managers who have processed a claim against their own policy because of an uninsured contractor understand why this verification matters.

General liability minimum coverage amounts for commercial cleaning operations vary by market, but policies below $1,000,000 per occurrence are generally considered insufficient for facilities above a certain size or value. Ask for the certificate of insurance, not a verbal assurance. The certificate shows the policy limits, the coverage period and the insurer. If the contractor cannot produce it within 24 hours, the policy may not exist or may have lapsed.

Workers' compensation

Workers' compensation coverage means that if a member of a cleaning crew is injured while working in your facility, the claim runs through the contractor's workers' compensation policy rather than through your insurance. In Arizona, employers with one or more employees are generally required to carry workers' compensation.

A cleaning contractor operating without workers' compensation is in violation of Arizona law, and the exposure to you as the facility owner or manager is significant: in some circumstances, workers' compensation liability for an uninsured contractor's employee can transfer to the party who contracted their services. This is not a theoretical risk; it has been litigated in Arizona courts.

Bonding

A commercial cleaning bond is a type of surety bond that provides financial recourse in the event that a crew member steals from a client's facility. Bonding is not the same as insurance: it covers a specific type of loss (employee theft) that general liability typically excludes.

For a cleaning contractor with recurring access to occupied commercial buildings, bonding is standard practice. The bond amount should be sufficient to cover the value of property accessible to the crew in your facility. Ask for the bond certificate along with the insurance certificate, and confirm the bond is current.

What to request before signing any contract

  • Certificate of general liability insurance (ask to be named as additional insured)
  • Proof of current workers' compensation coverage
  • Bond certificate and bond amount
  • Confirmation of how long the contractor has been in operation

A contractor who delays, deflects or is unable to provide these documents quickly is a contractor whose coverage deserves scrutiny. Established, properly insured contractors can produce these documents within 24 hours without difficulty.
