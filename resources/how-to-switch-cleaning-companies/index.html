How to Switch Commercial Cleaning Companies Without Downtime

The practical steps for transitioning from one cleaning contractor to another in Tempe—without a service gap or a dispute over your existing agreement.

Why switching is harder than it looks

Switching commercial cleaning companies is one of the more logistically fussy facility management tasks. It involves coordinating two contractors around the same physical space, managing key and access credential handoffs, and timing the transition so there is no night where neither contractor shows up and no night where both try to. Facilities managers who have done it once know the steps; those who have not often underestimate the lead time required.

This guide covers the practical steps for a commercial cleaning transition in Tempe. The sequence applies whether you are leaving a company because of performance, price or relationship reasons.

Step 1: Read your current contract before you do anything else

Most commercial cleaning contracts have a termination notice clause, typically 30 or 60 days. Some require notice in writing; some require notice sent to a specific address or contact. Before you begin any conversation with a new contractor, know your notice period and the method required.

If your current contract auto-renews, check the auto-renewal window. Some contracts renew for 12-month terms with a 60-day window before renewal in which you must provide notice to prevent automatic renewal. Missing that window by a week can lock you in for another year.

Your current contract also governs what happens to keys, access credentials and any supplies the contractor stores at your facility. Know this before you ask for them back.

Step 2: Get a new quote before you give notice

Do not give notice to your current contractor until you have a signed agreement with a new one. Facilities that give notice first and then solicit quotes often find themselves in a compressed timeline where they either accept an inferior new contractor or negotiate a problematic extension with the contractor they just terminated.

When soliciting a new quote, provide the new contractor with a copy of your current scope of work if you have one. If your current contractor operates without a written scope, document what the service includes before the new contractor walks the facility. This becomes the baseline for the new scope.

Step 3: Agree on an overlap period

The safest transition structure is a brief overlap period where the new contract is active for one to two weeks before the existing contract terminates. During this period, the new contractor performs a walkthrough and shadows the current schedule without executing cleaning work. This allows them to understand the facility before they take over sole responsibility.

In the Tempe market, this is a common request and most established contractors can accommodate it. If a new contractor is unwilling to schedule a pre-transition walkthrough, that is a data point about how they operate.

Step 4: Manage the access handoff explicitly

Access credential handoffs are where transitions most often go wrong. Keys, fobs, codes and security PINs need to move from the outgoing contractor to you (not to the incoming contractor directly) on a documented date. You then issue new credentials to the incoming contractor, which allows you to change credentials in between if the outgoing contractor's copies are unaccounted for.

Do not assume the outgoing contractor will return all copies of your keys. Confirm the count at the start of the contract and reconcile it at termination. If copies are missing, change the affected locks or codes before the new contractor begins.

Step 5: Schedule the first clean and set expectations

The first clean under a new contractor is the highest-risk visit. Brief your new contractor specifically on the areas of the facility where the prior service fell short—this is where performance problems that prompted the switch are most likely to recur if not explicitly addressed. Confirm the first visit in writing, including who is on the crew and what the scope covers.

Schedule a review after the first two weeks. Do not wait 90 days to assess whether the transition was successful.
